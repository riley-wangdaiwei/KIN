# Sole proprietor

https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/strategy/sole-proprietorship-vs-llc

Sole proprietor v. LLC

https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/174u41g/do_i_need_to_establish_a_business_before_selling/

You need a sales certificate from your state but you can be a sole proprietor.

In the US, you can obtain a business bank account as this sole proprietor. Sole proprietors can also create a fictitious business name by following the appropriate steps in their jurisdiction. You don't need to create an LLC or corporation to have a business bank account.

With regard registering as an LLC and having employees, let me clarify something: the reason you would want to register your business as a corporation (C, S, LLC, or LLP) is to shield yourself (shareholders) from personal liability arising from any legal action related to your business. It has nothing whatsoever to do with having employees. You can be a sole proprietorship and have employees; you can be an LLC and have no employees. The form of business has no bearing on whether you can/should have employees. It's all about legal (and tax) liabilities.

“I wouldn't worry about an llc until you start making at least a couple thousand a month profit. You don't need a lawyer. Just get started and see if you get traction and make sales.

If you are going to invest a significant amount of money up front, then an llc may make more sense.

You don't need an EIN.

Just copy privacy and cookie policy from a reputable website.

I would recommend opening a separate bank account under your name and have all business expenses and income go through that account.

I have been operating a successful online business for almost 10 years. This is exactly what I did.”

Verified NC decision guide (October 2026)

First decide ownership, not headcount. A sole proprietorship has exactly one owner. A second person may be an employee or contractor, but if both founders own the business, call it a two-owner venture: the usual choices are a general partnership or a two-member LLC. The IRS normally taxes a two-member domestic LLC as a partnership unless it elects corporate treatment. A general partnership is simpler to start but the partners generally face personal liability; an LLC usually provides liability separation and requires state filings. Sources: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/ | https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/entities

Practical choice for this team

If both people will contribute capital, make decisions and share ownership/profits, compare a two-member LLC with a general partnership. For an online store taking customer payments, a two-member LLC is usually the stronger starting option when the founders want personal liability separation and clear ownership rules. It costs more and requires an annual report. Do not describe a jointly owned business as a sole proprietorship merely because one person runs checkout. If one person truly owns 100% and the other is only paid for work, a sole proprietorship can be used for a small, low-risk test; the owner personally bears business debts and claims. Record the real arrangement in writing. Sources: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/ | https://www.sosnc.gov/frequently_asked_questions/by_title/_BRD_FAQ_Annual_Reports

If one owner chooses sole proprietorship in North Carolina

No LLC formation filing with the NC Secretary of State is needed when operating under the individual's legal name. If using a store name other than the owner's legal name, file an Assumed Business Name certificate with the county Register of Deeds; this is a name filing, not liability protection or an exclusive trademark. Then check product and local permits, register with NCDOR for sales and use tax before taxable retail sales when required, and get an EIN if IRS rules or banking/employee needs call for one. NC has no single generic business license. Sources: https://www.sosnc.gov/manual/launching_a_business/register_your_business | https://www.sosnc.gov/frequently_asked_questions/by_title_direct/_Assumed_Business_Names | https://www.nc.gov/working/business-nc/start-my-business | https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/sales-and-use-tax/sales-and-use-tax-registration

If both owners choose an NC LLC

Choose an available name and NC registered agent; file Articles of Organization with NC Secretary of State ($125 state filing fee); sign a two-member operating agreement covering ownership, contributions, decisions, profit sharing and exits; obtain an EIN; register for applicable NC taxes and permits; open a business bank account; and file the NC LLC annual report (due April 15 each year after formation). Sources: https://www.sosnc.gov/manual/launching_a_business/register_your_business | https://www.sosnc.gov/fees/by_title/_business_registration | https://www.sosnc.gov/frequently_asked_questions/by_title/_BRD_FAQ_Annual_Reports

Corrections to the Reddit notes above

“Wait until several thousand dollars of monthly profit” is an opinion, not a legal threshold for forming an LLC. “You don't need an EIN” depends on the structure, employees and tax rules. Do not copy another site's privacy or cookie policy: describe the data collection and tools actually used by this store. Non-US citizenship does not itself decide the entity type, but each founder must check whether their immigration status authorizes their planned work in the US; forming an entity does not grant work authorization. Sources: https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/entities | https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security | https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-10-part-a-chapter-2

# LLC

LLC and funding overview

Decision summary

For a small online retail business run from North Carolina, an LLC is often a practical starting structure: it separates business obligations from members' personal assets, allows flexible management, and can have one or multiple owners. Protection is not absolute: personal guarantees, misconduct, and failure to keep business affairs separate can still create exposure. An LLC is formed under state law; its federal tax treatment depends on owner count and elections. Sources: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/ | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc

What an LLC suits

Suitable when founders want liability separation and operational flexibility for a small or growing store. A sole proprietorship is simpler but does not create a separate legal entity. A corporation can be preferable when a venture expects institutional equity investors or a more standardized share structure. A two-founder LLC needs a written operating agreement covering ownership percentages, contributions, decisions, profit distributions, departures, and disputes. Source: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/

Advantages and tradeoffs

Advantages: separate legal entity and generally limited member liability; flexible number of members and management; default pass-through federal tax treatment for a multi-member LLC, unless it elects corporate treatment. 

Tradeoffs: filing and annual-report costs; bookkeeping and state tax duties; members may owe self-employment tax depending on classification and circumstances; investor admission needs careful agreement drafting. A one-member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income tax; a domestic LLC with two or more members defaults to partnership treatment. Source: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc

LLC versus corporation and federal taxes

Legal form and tax treatment are separate choices. An LLC is formed under state law, but the IRS can tax it as a disregarded entity, partnership or corporation. A corporation normally uses C-corporation tax treatment unless it qualifies and elects S-corporation status. The InCorp comparison is a useful introduction; its statements that every LLC owner pays 15.3% self-employment tax on all profits and that either structure can always elect S status are too broad for your situation. Sources: https://www.incorp.com/resources/knowledge-base/llc-and-corporation | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership

Default LLC taxation: a one-member domestic LLC is generally disregarded for federal income tax; an individual owner usually reports business activity on their own return. A domestic LLC with two or more members defaults to partnership tax treatment: it generally files Form 1065 and issues Schedule K-1 to members, who report their shares of income. Active owners may owe self-employment tax under applicable rules. Income can be taxable to owners even if cash stays in the business. Source: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership

C corporation taxation: the corporation files Form 1120 and pays income tax on its taxable profit. When after-tax earnings are paid as dividends, shareholders generally pay tax on those dividends, creating two levels of tax. Wages paid to working owners are handled through payroll rules, which is different from dividends. A C corporation can retain profits for growth, but that does not automatically make it tax-cheaper overall. Sources: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/corporations-raising-capital-and-distributing-profits-youtube-video-text-script | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/paying-yourself

S corporation taxation: a qualifying corporation or LLC may elect pass-through S status; an LLC can elect C-corporation treatment with Form 8832, while an eligible S election generally uses Form 2553. S status has shareholder restrictions, including no nonresident-alien shareholders. Non-US citizenship alone does not settle whether a founder is a resident or nonresident alien for tax purposes, so confirm each founder's tax residency before treating this as an option. Sources: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4 | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporations

Decision for this business: compare an LLC taxed under its default rules with an LLC electing corporate treatment and a corporation using realistic forecasts for profit, owner pay, reinvestment and distributions. Include North Carolina taxes and any foreign-owner reporting or withholding. Have a cross-border CPA check the numbers before making a tax election. Sources: https://www.incorp.com/resources/knowledge-base/llc-and-corporation |https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc

Non-US citizen founders

The IRS says most states do not restrict LLC ownership by foreign individuals or entities. Forming or owning an LLC does not itself provide immigration status or permission to perform work in the United States. Each founder should check their actual visa or employment authorization before actively running the business; tax residency is a separate question from citizenship. S-corporation tax status is generally unavailable if any shareholder is a nonresident alien. Sources: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporations | https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states

Funding options and decision

Start with a budget for inventory, platform, marketing, fulfillment, tax, and returns; estimate cash needed until collections cover costs. Options to compare: founder funds or family investment; standard bank or credit-union credit; supplier terms; customer preorders or reward crowdfunding; and private equity investment. Debt preserves ownership but requires repayment; equity dilutes ownership; crowdfunding or preorders create delivery obligations. SBA says federal grants are generally not available simply to start a business. Sources: https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/ | https://www.sba.gov/counseling/grow-your-business/

Critical 2026 funding eligibility

SBA announced 2026 citizenship rules for SBA-backed 7(a), 504 and Microloan programs. According to SBA, a business owned in whole or in part by foreign nationals is ineligible; owners must be US citizens or US nationals with principal US residence. Because you are not US citizens, do not assume SBA-backed lending is available. Ask lenders about conventional products and eligibility for your exact status. Sources: https://legacy.sba.gov/article/2026/03/09/sba-bans-foreign-nationals-accessing-sba-backed-loans | https://legacy.sba.gov/document/procedural-notice-5000-876626-revised-applicant-ownership-citizenship-residency-requirements-7a-504-loans

Meeting decisions

Confirm founders' visa/work authorization and tax residency; product category and target countries; whether there will be one or multiple owners; startup budget and funding amount; and whether EU customers will be targeted. These answers determine tax filings, licenses, product rules, and website privacy obligations.

# Webpage

Ecommerce website: content and launch map

Scope

Use this page to plan the customer-facing site. The US regulations and EU regulations tabs explain rules that depend on where the store operates and sells. Check product-specific requirements separately.

Recommended navigation and placement

Footer: Contact / About | Privacy Policy | Terms of Sale | Shipping & Delivery | Returns & Refunds. Link relevant policies again from product pages and checkout. Show material costs and limits before the customer places an order.

Source:https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/alt067-electronic-commerce-selling-internationally-guide-businesses.pdf

Product pages and checkout

For each product, show an accurate description, price, variants, availability, material restrictions and any recurring charge. At checkout, show the total price, taxes and shipping charges, delivery estimate, accepted payment methods and a clear order button. Ensure checkout language matches the policy pages.

Source:https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/alt067-electronic-commerce-selling-internationally-guide-businesses.pdf

What each policy page should answer

Privacy Policy: What personal data is collected? Why? Which vendors receive it? How long is it kept? How can customers contact the business or make applicable privacy requests?

Terms of Sale: Who is the seller? When is an order accepted? How are payment, pricing errors, taxes, cancellations, warranties and disputes handled? A separate terms page is a practical baseline for a store accepting orders; exact legal duties vary.

Shipping & Delivery: Where do you ship? What are processing and delivery estimates, charges, tracking methods, and procedures for delays or lost packages?

Returns & Refunds: Which products qualify? What is the return window and condition? Who pays return shipping? When and how are refunds issued? What happens with damaged or incorrect items?

Contact / About: Give customers a reliable way to identify and reach the seller. About content can explain the brand; Contact should make service questions and policy requests easy.

Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security | https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule

Data inventory before writing the Privacy Policy

List all data actually handled: name, email, phone, billing and delivery details, order history, contact-form messages, IP/device logs, cookies, analytics and advertising identifiers. Map each purpose, vendor (hosting/CDN, payment processor, shipping carrier, email platform, analytics, fonts or scripts), storage period and customer-request path. Check vendor settings and policies; a template or generator must be corrected to match the live site.

Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security

Technology and jurisdiction checks

“Impressum” is associated with some European disclosure regimes; it is not a standard name for a universal US page. A blanket claim that every US shop needs a cookie banner, or that cookieless analytics never needs a privacy notice, is unreliable. Review the actual tools, visitors and applicable state/country rules. IP addresses and server logs belong in the data inventory.

Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security | https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/growing/digitalising/online-privacy/index_en.htm

Pre-launch walkthrough

Place a test order on desktop and mobile. Check product claims, final charges, tax calculation, delivery promise, confirmation email, cancellation, returns, customer contact, privacy-request route and accessible forms/checkout. Revisit policies when adding vendors, products or new markets.

Sources:https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule | https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/
